Good Rhythms

What makes a rhythm “good”?

In The Geometry of Musical Rhythm, Godfried Toussaint drew a rhythm as a polygon inside a circle and looked for geometric properties shared by well-known rhythms. Paul Lascabettes and Isabelle Bloch turned twelve of those properties into formulas that give each one a score between 0 and 1. A rhythm’s score is the sum of the twelve, so it lies between 0 and 12.

The app scores every rhythm with k onsets over n pulses that starts on pulse 1. There are C(n−1, k−1) of them. Nine of the properties are min-max normalized over that whole set. Symmetry, shadow and fractal are used as they are. A score is therefore always relative to its (k, n).

For k = 5 and n = 16, the son clave comes out on top, as Toussaint predicted. Other winners include the tresillo (3, 8), fume-fume (5, 12), samba (7, 16) and Steve Reich’s Clapping Music rhythm (8, 12).

How to read the pictures

Polygon notation: pulses run clockwise from the top, and onsets are the coloured dots. When a rhythm plays, a hand sweeps around the circle. Box notation: one cell per pulse, with onsets filled. Shaded cells mark the start of each beat.

Implementation notes

  • Pulses are numbered 1 to n here. The paper numbers them 0 to n − 1, so its son clave {0, 3, 6, 10, 12} appears here as {1, 4, 7, 11, 13}.
  • Every value in the paper’s Table 1 is reproduced, along with the top 20 of Figure 2 and the top three of Figure 3 for (3, 8), (5, 12) and (7, 16). For (8, 12), Reich’s rhythm comes first as in the paper, but the paper’s 2nd place comes 6th here.
  • Table 1’s “Entropy H” and “Entropy D” rows are swapped relative to their definitions. This does not affect the totals.
  • Fractal: the dilation l·x must land inside the cycle (it does not wrap around to the first pulse).
  • Oddity is undefined for odd n. For prime n, closure and aperture take the same value for every rhythm. Properties like these are left out, and the maximum score drops to match.
  • Exact scoring is limited to 2,000,000 rhythms, which covers every k for n ≤ 24.

Playback

Tempo is in beats per minute. A beat is 4 pulses when n is a multiple of 4 (but not of 3), 3 pulses when n is a multiple of 3, and 2 pulses otherwise. The metronome clicks on each beat and accents pulse 1. On iPhone, the ring/silent switch may mute sound in older iOS versions.

Reference
